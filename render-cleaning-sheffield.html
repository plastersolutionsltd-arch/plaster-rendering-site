Quick Answer

Can green render be cleaned? Yes. On silicone and monocouche render we use a soft wash — low pressure and a biocide that kills the algae and mould at the root — not a pressure washer, which can scour the finish and drive water in. A biocide is left on to keep regrowth back, a protective coat goes on after, and if cleaning alone will not bring it back, we can re-colour it.

Why render goes green (or black)

The green is algae and moss; black patches and streaks are usually mould and dirt washed down from sills and gutters. All of it grows where a wall stays damp: north-facing elevations, walls shaded by trees or the house next door, and anywhere water runs down the face.

Even silicone render, which is sold as self-cleaning, can grow algae on a wall that rarely dries out. It does not mean the render has failed — but left alone, the growth holds moisture against the surface and the wall only gets worse.

Why we soft wash, not pressure wash

A pressure washer removes what you can see and leaves the root behind. It can also scour the coloured surface of a monocouche, open up hairline cracks and force water behind the render. The growth comes back sooner, and the wall can end up worse than it started.

A soft wash works the other way round. The biocide kills the algae, moss and mould where they are rooted, and a low-pressure rinse takes them away. The finish is left as it was, just clean.

When cleaning is not enough

Sometimes the staining has gone into the colour, or the render underneath is cracked or hollow and letting water in. Cleaning a wall like that only treats the symptom. We will tell you what we find, re-colour the render where that is the answer, and quote any repairs separately so you can choose.
